Why roofs fail up here

A roof in Buxton is not a roof in Bolton.

At 1,000 ft, Buxton is one of the highest market towns in England. What passes as a fine specification in the valleys gets taken apart by a Peak District winter. Four things drive nearly every failure we get called to.

Exposure

Severe wind-driven rain

Most of the High Peak sits in BS 5534 exposure zone 4–5. That changes headlap, batten gauge and fixing spec — every slate mechanically fixed, not nail-and-hope. Get it wrong and rain tracks uphill under the covering.

Freeze–thaw

Water that expands inside the tile

We get 60–80 frost days a year. Porous concrete tiles and soft bricks take on water, freeze, and delaminate from the face. It is the single most common reason a 30-year roof only lasts 18 up here.

Movement

Old gritstone walls that shift

Rubble-filled stone walls and softwood structures move. Rigid mortar bedding cracks, ridges lift, valleys open up. We dry-fix ridges and hips as standard and re-point in a lime mortar that flexes with the building.

Planning

National Park & conservation rules

Inside the Peak District National Park and its conservation areas, replacement usually has to be like-for-like — reclaimed stone slate or natural slate, matched coursing, matched ridge. We source it and handle the specification.

Choosing a covering

What each roof covering actually gives you.

Straight numbers, no sales patter. Minimum pitch matters because a covering laid below its rated pitch will let water back regardless of who fits it — and up here, weight and porosity matter as much as price.

CoveringService lifeMin. pitchWeightBest suited to
Stone slate100+ yrs30°90–150 kg/m²Listed and National Park properties; like-for-like reclaimed gritstone
Natural slate80–100 yrs20°25–35 kg/m²Most Peak District houses; Welsh or Spanish, light enough for old structures
Lead flat100+ yrs1:80 fall30–45 kg/m²Bay tops, porches, valleys and anywhere you want it done once
Plain tile60–80 yrs35°65–78 kg/m²Steeper traditional roofs; clay outlasts concrete in frost
Interlocking tile40–60 yrs17.5°45–52 kg/m²Budget-conscious re-roofs and shallower pitches; fastest to lay
GRP fibreglass25–30 yrs1:80 fall4–6 kg/m²Dormers, extensions and garages; seamless, no joints to fail
EPDM rubber25–35 yrs1:80 fall2–3 kg/m²Large single-span flat roofs laid in one sheet
Torch-on felt15–20 yrs1:80 fall5–8 kg/m²Outbuildings and short-term budget repairs

Service life assumes correct specification, adequate ventilation and Peak District exposure. Concrete tiles at the bottom of their range are almost always frost damage, not age.
